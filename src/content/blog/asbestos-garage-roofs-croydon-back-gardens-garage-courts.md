Go down the side passage of almost any interwar semi in Shirley or Selsdon and there's a decent chance the garage at the end has a grey, ribbed roof that nobody has touched since the 1970s. Asbestos cement went onto British garages, sheds and lean-tos for most of the last century, and it carried on until the UK banned the last type of asbestos in 1999. Any sheet roof older than that is a suspect until a test says otherwise. Asbestos garage roofs in Croydon have two local complications. Access is the first: a lot of them sit a whole back garden away from the road, behind houses with no side gate. Disposal is the second. None of the borough's three tips will take the sheets, but most households can have the first 15 square metres collected for nothing. Below is how to spot one, what not to do to it, who can take it off, where it goes afterwards and what the new roof costs.

## Where asbestos garage roofs turn up across Croydon

The borough's housing gives you a fairly reliable map. In the interwar suburbs to the south and east (Shirley, Selsdon, Sanderstead, Coulsdon) most semis have a side drive running to a detached garage. Plenty of those garages arrived after the war rather than with the house, and many were precast concrete sectional garages, delivered as a kit and bolted together on site. The usual roof on those kits was corrugated asbestos cement laid on light purlins.

<img src="/images/roof-repairs-croydon-loc-shirley-flat-roof-rear-extension-garden-trees-w800.webp" alt="Black and white aerial view of two rows of terraced houses with long narrow back gardens, many with sheds and outbuildings at the far end" width="800" height="600" loading="lazy" />

North of the town centre it looks different. The Victorian and Edwardian terraces of Thornton Heath, Norbury, South Norwood and Addiscombe rarely had space for a garage, so the sheets show up on coal stores, old outside toilets, lean-to workshops and the occasional garage at the bottom of a garden that backs onto a rear alley. Then there are the postwar estates such as New Addington and Fieldway, where rows of lock-up garages share a single court and, quite often, a single run of roof.

One quick sorting question before going further. If your garage roof is flat, has a timber deck and is covered in felt, it is not asbestos sheeting at all. That is an ordinary [flat roof repair](/flat-roof-repairs) job and nothing below applies to it.

## Is it cement sheet? Checking without a ladder

You can tell a lot from an upstairs back window and the zoom on your phone. Asbestos cement sheets are grey when new and weather to a dull, speckled grey-green, usually with patches of yellow and white lichen. They are thick, about 6mm, with big rounded corrugations, and the chipped corners and edges look more like stone or stiff biscuit than metal. Underneath, inside the garage, you'll typically see hook bolts wrapped around the purlins with domed washers on top.

<img src="/images/blog/moss-cushions-weathered-cement-sheet-roof-croydon.webp" alt="Cushions of green moss growing on a weathered, gritty grey roof surface" width="900" height="480" loading="lazy" />

The things that rule it out are just as useful. Steel sheets are thin, ring when tapped and go rusty at the laps. Bitumen sheets, sold under names like Onduline, are soft and slightly floppy, and come in black, green or red. Plastic and polycarbonate let light through. A garage built or re-roofed after 2000 is very unlikely to have asbestos on it, though "the previous owner said it was replaced" is not the same as a receipt.

None of this proves anything either way. It tells you whether to spend money on a test, which is the next step.

## Moss, gutters and a pressure washer: three jobs to leave alone

Croydon's southern half is heavily wooded, and a garage roof under an oak or a sycamore in Purley or Kenley grows moss in thick cushions and fills its gutter every autumn. That is exactly when people reach for the wrong tools.

<img src="/images/blog/brick-outbuilding-corrugated-roof-timber-doors-croydon.webp" alt="Single-storey brick outbuilding with three timber doors under a green-painted corrugated roof" width="900" height="490" loading="lazy" />

The pressure washer comes first on the list. Weathering has already loosened the top surface of an old cement sheet, and a jet blasts that surface off and sprays the slurry across your lawn, the patio and probably next door's washing. HSE's guidance on cleaning these roofs says high-pressure jetting is for exceptional circumstances only. The safe approach is to wet the moss and lift it off by hand with a scraper from a proper access platform, and only if it really has to go.

Walking on it is the second. HSE treats every asbestos cement roof as fragile, with no exceptions. Sheets that have sat out for fifty winters snap without warning, and the drop is onto a concrete floor or the car.

The third is drilling, screwing or cutting into it to fit a bracket, a light, an aerial or a new section of gutter. Power tools are the quickest way to turn a stable sheet into airborne dust, and an angle grinder is the worst of the lot. If a sheet is intact and you have no reason to touch it, leaving it where it is remains a perfectly sensible choice. If you or a tradesperson do need to work on one, <a href="https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/essentials/index.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HSE's Asbestos essentials task sheets</a> set out the method for each job, from gutter cleaning to taking a sheet off.

## Getting a sample tested before anybody quotes

A test costs very little next to the job itself, and it changes how the work is priced, who can do it and where the waste can go. There are two ways to get one. A surveyor can visit, wet a corner of a sheet, take a small piece, seal the broken edge and send the sample off. Or you can buy a postal kit, take the sample yourself following its instructions, double-bag it and post it to the lab. Use a lab accredited by UKAS for asbestos analysis either way. Results usually come back within a few working days.

<img src="/images/blog/lab-microscope-sample-analysis-asbestos-test-croydon.webp" alt="Technician in a face mask adjusting a microscope over a sample slide, with a pen and paperwork beside it" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" />

While you're in the garage, look up. Some garages and outbuildings have flat boards fixed under the roof or lining the walls, and on older ones that board can be asbestos insulating board rather than cement sheet. Insulating board is softer, breaks up more easily and is much more hazardous, and HSE says only a licensed contractor should remove it. If anything inside looks like board, get it sampled at the same time.

## Licensed, non-licensed or doing it yourself

Most work on asbestos cement can be done without an HSE licence. That doesn't mean anyone can do it. The people doing non-licensed work still need training in asbestos and the right equipment, and they have to follow a set method: wet the sheets, unbolt them rather than smash them, lower them rather than drop them, and wrap them before they leave the roof. A contractor who plans to break the sheets up and throw them in a skip is the one to avoid.

<img src="/images/blog/worker-white-coverall-respirator-protective-kit-croydon.webp" alt="Black and white photo of a worker in a white disposable coverall, gloves and respirator using a long-handled tool on a window indoors" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" />

What about doing it yourself? The asbestos regulations are written around people working for a living, and HSE's line for householders is simply that trained professionals should do it. Some people still take a small shed roof off themselves, and on a little lean-to that's understandable. If you do, pick a still, damp day, tell the neighbours, wear a disposable coverall and an FFP3 mask, keep the sheets wet, cut the bolts with hand tools and keep every sheet whole. It's a two-person job on anything bigger than a shed, and nobody should be standing on the sheets while they work.

If you hire someone, ask three things before you pay a deposit. Have their operatives had asbestos awareness and non-licensed work training, and can they show you the certificates? Are they a registered waste carrier? You can check on the Environment Agency's public register in a couple of minutes. And where will the sheets go, with what paperwork? Hazardous waste a contractor takes away should come with a consignment note.

## Disposing of an asbestos garage roof in Croydon

This is where Croydon differs from a lot of the country. Factory Lane, Fishers Farm and Purley Oaks all list asbestos as something they don't accept, so there is no driving it to the tip yourself. Don't put it in the black bin or a general builders' skip either.

<img src="/images/blog/crumpled-heavy-polythene-sheet-wrapping-croydon.webp" alt="Crumpled sheet of heavy polythene photographed against a black background" width="900" height="858" loading="lazy" />

The council sends householders to the hazardous waste service run by the City of London Corporation, which collects for every London borough except Hillingdon. <a href="https://www.croydon.gov.uk/environment/pollution/asbestos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Croydon Council's asbestos page</a> explains the arrangement, and the details on the <a href="https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/services/waste-and-recycling/hazardous-waste-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">City of London's hazardous waste collection page</a> are the ones to plan around:

- The first 15 square metres of sheet are collected free, paid for by the council. Above that you get a quote, and you pay before the collection.
- You get one collection per rolling year, from your home address, and you'll need to show recent council tax payments.
- Every sheet or piece has to be wrapped on its own in heavy-duty plastic and sealed with strong tape. The crew won't test, dismantle or wrap anything for you.
- Broken pieces go in sealed builders' rubble bags that one person can lift. The City's page says seven bags; Croydon's own page still says five half-filled ones, so check when you book.
- It can take up to ten working days from receiving your paperwork to arrange the pickup, and the phone line is voicemail only.
- Landlords, charities and businesses can't use it.

That 15 square metre limit is tighter than it sounds. A single garage roof measuring about 2.6 by 5.5 metres comes out at roughly 14 to 17 square metres of sheet once you count the overlaps and the overhang, so it may land just under the limit or just over it. A double garage goes over every time.

Think about the route, too. A wrapped 2.4 metre sheet is an awkward carry for two people along a side passage, and the job is worse through a terraced house with no back access. Work out where the stack will sit before the first bolt comes out. If you hire a contractor, disposal normally goes on their bill rather than through the City scheme. Make sure it does, because household waste you hand to someone unregistered who then dumps it can still land the fine on you.

## What goes back on, and what the job costs in Croydon

Once the old sheets are off, a single garage usually gets one of three roofs. Non-asbestos fibre cement sheets look almost identical to the old ones and sit on the same purlins, which suits anyone who wants the garage to look unchanged from the street. Box-profile steel is lighter and cheaper. Ask for an anti-condensation backing, or the underside will drip on the car every cold morning. And if the roof is nearly flat, a new timber deck with EPDM rubber or GRP on top turns it into a proper flat roof with a long life.

Paperwork is light. A detached garage under 30 square metres with no sleeping accommodation is normally exempt from building regulations, and re-roofing an outbuilding in a similar material rarely needs planning permission. The exception is a garage visible from the street in one of Croydon's conservation areas. Check with the council before you change how it looks.

## Garage courts, rented lock-ups and selling a house with one

Shared garage blocks need more organising than a single garage. If you rent a lock-up from the council or a housing association, the roof isn't yours to fix: report it to the landlord and leave it alone. If you own one garage in a privately owned row, the sheets often run across the party walls, so taking off your bit can expose the edge of next door's. It's worth getting the neighbours on side and using one contractor for the whole row. Remember too that the free collection comes from your home address, not the garage court round the corner, so sheets from a lock-up need a plan of their own.
