From the top of Westow Hill on a clear morning you can pick out the towers of the City and Canary Wharf. That view comes at a price, and the roofs pay it. Upper Norwood, Crystal Palace and the slopes running down to Selhurst sit on the Norwood Ridge, around 110 metres above sea level, which puts them among the highest neighbourhoods in London. Weather that brushes past a house in the Wandle valley hits these roofs head on. The housing adds its own puzzles: big Victorian villas built after the Crystal Palace was moved to Sydenham Hill in 1854, most now split into flats; long terraces round Selhurst Park; and postwar blocks built on old villa plots. Then there are the boundaries, because four London boroughs meet at a single road junction up here. If you're looking for roofers in Upper Norwood, Crystal Palace or Selhurst, this guide covers what tends to go wrong, which council has a say, what the paperwork involves and what the work costs in 2026.

## What 110 metres does to a roof in Upper Norwood

Wind gets faster as it climbs over a hill, and the Norwood Ridge has nothing in front of it to the south-west but a long run of suburbs and the North Downs. So the parts of a roof that catch the wind first, which are the verges, the top courses of slate, the ridge, the hips and the lead round a chimney, work harder here than on the same house built a mile downhill. Rain doesn't fall straight on an exposed corner of Church Road or Beulah Hill either. It gets driven sideways, under the laps of slates that would shed it on a calmer site.

<img src="/images/roof-repairs-croydon-site-brick-chimney-pots-slate-roof-croydon-w1200.webp" alt="Two brick chimney stacks with metal cowls rising through a grey slate roof under a pale, wind-streaked sky" width="900" height="600" loading="lazy" />

Height also changes how a roof should be fixed. BS 5534, the British Standard for slating and tiling, has the roofer work out fixings from the site's wind exposure, and both altitude and the shape of the land raise that figure. On the ridge that usually means centre-nailed slates and every ridge and hip tile mechanically fixed rather than sitting in mortar alone. If something has already blown off, our [storm damage repairs](/storm-damage-repairs) start by making the roof watertight, and the proper refix follows once the weather allows. When you collect quotes up here, ask each roofer how they would fix the new work. A vague answer tells you something.

## Church Road and Beulah Hill villas: the roof you can't see from the pavement

The grandest houses went up on Church Road, Beulah Hill, Harold Road and the streets between them in the second half of the 1800s, built for families drawn up the hill by the Palace and its trains. Croydon's own appraisal of the Harold Road conservation area describes the street as large Victorian villas built for exactly that crowd. They are three storeys, often with a semi-basement, Welsh slate on the main roof, tall stacks carrying six or eight flues, and the odd turret or steep gable thrown in for show.

<img src="/images/blog/london-rooftops-chimneys-hazy-sky-from-norwood.webp" alt="Red brick rooftops and chimney stacks in the foreground with the London skyline fading into haze behind" width="900" height="351" loading="lazy" />

The trouble usually sits out of sight. A lot of these roofs are not one simple pitch. Several slopes drain into a central valley, or into a lead-lined box gutter that runs across the house behind a parapet before it reaches a hopper on the back wall. Lead on roofs of this age was often laid in lengths too long to expand and contract freely, so it cracks along the folds after decades of summers and winters. Beaulieu Heights and the other old woodland pockets nearby drop leaves into those hidden gutters every autumn. When one of them blocks, water backs up over the lead and finds a joist. The first sign is usually a brown ring on a ceiling halfway across a top-floor flat, nowhere near an outside wall.

## One villa, five flats: getting the roof fixed when you own a slice

Very few of those villas are still single houses. Most were divided into flats during the twentieth century, and many now have a residents' company or a share-of-freehold arrangement running the building. Under nearly every lease the roof is shared structure. The top-floor owner gets the drips, but the freeholder or the company commissions the repair, and the cost is split between the flats through the service charge. Exactly how a lease divides that is set out in our guide to [who pays for roof repairs in Croydon's converted flats](/blog/who-pays-roof-repairs-croydon-converted-flats).

<img src="/images/blog/brick-block-of-flats-sash-windows-upper-norwood.webp" alt="Black and white view of a tall brick building of flats with rows of white sash windows" width="660" height="440" loading="lazy" />

Up here the practical side is what slows things down. A villa roof needs scaffold on at least one elevation, often two, so even a modest repair runs to four figures. If any one leaseholder's share is likely to go over £250, the freeholder has to consult everyone before the work is let, and that takes weeks. Report a leak in writing with photos straight away, and ask for a temporary fix in the meantime: a slate slid back into place, a sheet over a split gutter or a cleared outlet. Also ask for one survey of the whole roof rather than a series of patches. On a big building with five owners, separate call-outs for each new drip cost far more over a few years than one planned job.

## Selhurst: terraces below the ridge, and match days

Walk downhill from South Norwood Hill towards Selhurst station and the villas give way to long two-storey terraces in London stock brick and red brick, mostly late Victorian and Edwardian. The ground flattens and the wind eases. The roof problems become the familiar south London ones: a slate front slope where the old nails have rusted through, a rear wing with its own small roof, and a valley or shared gutter where the back slopes of two houses meet. Plenty of these houses have had loft conversions too, and the joint where a dormer cheek meets the old slate is a regular source of leaks.

<img src="/images/blog/floodlight-towers-blue-sky-selhurst.webp" alt="Bank of floodlights on tall white towers against a clear blue sky" width="900" height="687" loading="lazy" />

What makes Selhurst different is the football ground. Selhurst Park has been Crystal Palace's home since 1924, and the streets round Holmesdale Road, Park Road and Whitehorse Lane fill with cars and fans on home match days. If your roofer needs a scaffold delivery, a skip swap or a van parked outside all day, book it for a day without a fixture. On planning, Selhurst has no conservation area of its own, so like-for-like re-roofing on a house needs no consent. Changing what a house roof looks like is covered by the national permitted development rules, while flats have no permitted development rights for roof alterations at all.

## Postwar blocks on old villa plots: flat roofs and shared bills

Between the villas of Upper Norwood stand plenty of blocks of flats from the 1950s, 60s and 70s, from two storeys up to towers, many built where a large Victorian house once stood. Most have flat roofs finished in built-up felt or asphalt, with parapets, a tank room or lift motor room on top, and rainwater outlets that drain through the building. Those roofs have usually been patched several times, and the original layers are reaching the end of their life.

<img src="/images/blog/postwar-concrete-flats-facade-upper-norwood.webp" alt="Pebbledash concrete panels and rows of windows on a postwar block of flats" width="720" height="480" loading="lazy" />

The faults are predictable. Water ponds where the deck has sagged, upstands crack where the roof meets a parapet or plant room, and outlets clog because nobody climbs up there between surveys. Before anyone strips an old roof on a block built before 2000, there should be an asbestos survey, since bitumen felts, roofing sheets and some coatings from that era can contain it. When more than half of a flat roof's area is re-covered, Building Regulations usually require the insulation to be brought up to current standards if that is practical, which changes the price and can raise the finished roof level. Because the freeholder or managing agent pays and recovers the money through the service charge, expect a specification, competing tenders and the same consultation rules as a converted house.

## Four boroughs at one junction: whose rules apply to your roof

The Crystal Palace Triangle, the shopping streets of Westow Hill, Westow Street and Church Road, has never had a single council in charge. Croydon, Bromley and Lambeth each look after one side of it. The junction at the top of Anerley Hill, near where the old Vicar's Oak boundary tree once stood, is where Croydon, Bromley, Lambeth and Southwark meet. Lewisham starts not far along Sydenham Hill. Two houses facing each other across a road can sit in different boroughs, with different planning officers and different conservation area rules.

<img src="/images/blog/hilltop-park-view-london-skyline-crystal-palace.webp" alt="Grassy hilltop park path with a walker and a dog, and the London skyline under grey cloud" width="900" height="675" loading="lazy" />

On the Croydon side there are four conservation areas to know. Harold Road was designated in 1973, Church Road in 1974 (it also takes in Beaulieu Heights, Sylvan Hill and Grange Hill), Upper Norwood Triangle in 1989 and Beulah Hill in 2008. Croydon's page for the <a href="https://www.croydon.gov.uk/planning-and-regeneration/planning/planning-policy/conservation-and-heritage/conservation-areas-directory/upper-norwood-triangle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Upper Norwood Triangle conservation area</a> notes that it adjoins Bromley's Crystal Palace Park conservation area and Lambeth's Westow Hill conservation area, and Lambeth's <a href="https://www.lambeth.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/conservation-and-listed-buildings/conservation-area-profiles/westow-hill-ca23" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Westow Hill conservation area profile</a> covers the commercial frontage on the north side of the hill. Repairs that match what is already there need no consent in any of them. The line falls at changes: swapping natural slate for something else on a front slope, adding rooflights or dormers that face the street, or altering a prominent stack. Several buildings in the Church Road area are listed too, and a listed building needs listed building consent for work that affects its character, even when the same work would be routine on the house next door.

## Scaffold licences and 2026 roof prices in Upper Norwood, Crystal Palace and Selhurst

On the ridge, access decides a big share of the bill. The villas are tall and often set above a basement area, the roads are steep, and Church Road, Westow Hill and Beulah Hill carry buses. A scaffold on the pavement needs a highway licence from whichever council runs that road. Croydon's <a href="https://www.croydon.gov.uk/business-licences-and-tenders/licences-permits-and-registrations/licence-and-permit-fees/licence-and-permit-fees/highways-and-street-trading-licence-fees" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">highways licence fee schedule</a> puts a scaffold at £488 per elevation for four weeks, or £610 over 10 metres long, plus a refundable bond of £50 per square metre of highway enclosed. On a slope the scaffold also needs levelled base plates and sole boards. It should be priced as its own line, which our article on [scaffolding and access in Croydon roof quotes](/blog/scaffolding-access-croydon-roof-quotes) explains in more detail.

Here are typical 2026 price ranges for this kind of work in south London, including VAT and, where a job normally needs it, scaffold:

- Refixing slipped or wind-lifted slates on an exposed slope, worked from roof ladders: £220 to £480
- Mechanically refixing a ridge that has lifted in a gale on a terrace or semi: £650 to £1,400
- Relining a central valley or box gutter in lead on a three-storey villa: £2,800 to £6,500
- Rebuilding a tall villa chimney stack above the roofline, with new lead flashings: £2,500 to £5,500
- Re-slating a two-storey Selhurst terrace in natural slate: £11,500 to £17,000
- Re-covering the flat roof of a small postwar block of around 150 square metres: £20,000 to £38,000, before the service-charge split
- Re-slating a large villa with new lead work throughout: £32,000 to £60,000

On the ridge, four things push a price up more than anything else: the height of the building, a highway licence that runs for weeks rather than days, a conservation officer asking for matching natural slate, and asbestos in an old flat roof. Ask for each one as a separate line, and ask where on the roof the quote was written from. A survey done from the pavement can't see a hidden valley.
